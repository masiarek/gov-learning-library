## CLASSIFICATIONS OF GOVERNMENT

- **UNITARY:** one body rules. Worries about too much power in one gov causing corruption, may cause an oppressive king.&nbsp;[⚠](../../CORRECTIONS.md#dates-and-precision)
- **CONFEDERATE:** power resides within smaller states / localized gov.
- **FEDERAL:** power split between bodies.

## ARTICLES OF CONFEDERATION

Created in 1781.&nbsp;[⚠](../../CORRECTIONS.md#dates-and-precision) Prior to this, there was WAR!!!!!

- each state has their own constitution and Bill of Rights. They varied in structure, each having different culture and ideas of how they should be governed, so when it came time to write the AoC, it was extremely difficult to agree.
- Despite this, the states had to band together in order to survive as a nation, to create the AoC.

**PURPOSE OF THE AOC:**

- leave state sovereignty in place
    - legislation passed w/ a 2/3 vote in congress (one vote per state)
    - amendments need 100% support from congress & state legislatures
    - Money can't be taken forcibly from the states
    - A standing Army can't be raised w/o support from states.
- Made due to a fear of a tyrannical government.

**PROBLEMS WITH THE ARTICLES OF CONFEDERATION: <u>WEAK</u>.**

Cultures of states impacted the national body.

- no central leadership
- congress has no power to enforce laws
- can't regulate trade
- no court system
    - If states have an issue w/ the fed gov, there's nowhere to go.
- changes to the AoC required unanimous consent from all 13 states, which, due to their many differences, all 13 rarely agreed. Nothing got done.

## SHAY'S REBELLION [*Shays'*]

- Farmers turned soldiers turned farmers, unpaid for war.
- **DANIEL SHAYS:**
    - Farmer; served in the American revolution
- Higher taxes than ever, no way to pay — Shays & pals revolt.
- No government to stop the rebellion — weakness in the AoC is revealed.
- Wealthy people hired mercenaries to stop it.
- Shays and troops were eventually pardoned.

## Corrections on this page

Nothing here is flat-out wrong. Two lines are loose enough to sharpen, and one name is misspelled throughout.

| The note | Sharper |
|---|---|
| Unitary "may cause an oppressive king" | That is the **fear**, not the definition. The UK, France and Japan are unitary *democracies*. Definition: the central government holds sovereignty; local units exercise power **delegated from it**, and it can take that power back. |
| AoC "created in 1781" | **Drafted and adopted by Congress November 1777**; **ratified and in force 1 March 1781**. Maryland was the last holdout, over western land claims — and that four-year gap is itself part of the story. |
| "each state has their own constitution and Bill of Rights" | Most did. **Connecticut and Rhode Island** kept modified colonial charters instead, and not every state attached a separate bill of rights. |
| "no court system" | True where it counted — **no national judiciary**. Article IX did allow courts for piracy and high-seas felonies, and made Congress last resort in **state-versus-state** disputes. That gap is the better example than "states have an issue with the fed gov". |
| "Wealthy people hired **mercenaries**" | **Boston merchants privately subscribed the funds** for a 4,400-man **state militia** under General Benjamin Lincoln. A public force paid for privately — because Congress could not raise one. Which is exactly the point. |
| **SHAY'S** · "Shay & pals" · "Shay and troops" | **Shays'** — his name is Daniel **Shays**, with an s. (The heading **DANIEL SHAYS** is right.) |

**Correct, in case of doubt:** "2/3 vote in congress (one vote per state)" ✓ — that is the nine-of-thirteen rule · "amendments need 100%" ✓ — unanimous consent of all thirteen legislatures · "Money can't be taken forcibly from the states" ✓ — requisitions only, no tax power · Shays as a Revolutionary War veteran ✓ · pardoned ✓ (June 1788, by Governor John Hancock).
