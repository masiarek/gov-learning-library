# Unit 1 — Foundations of American Democracy

[← All units](../README.md)

Where American government comes from, and why it is shaped the way it is. The unit runs on one question: **how do you build a government strong enough to work, but not strong enough to become the thing you just fought a war to escape?** Every topic below is somebody's answer to it.

**The storyline:** a war leaves Britain in debt → Britain taxes the colonies → the colonies object that taxation without representation is not legitimate → they declare independence on a theory of natural rights → they build a deliberately weak government (the Articles) → it is too weak → a farmers' revolt proves it → they try again in Philadelphia.

## What the College Board tests in this unit

Topics 1.1 through 1.9. Beyond the material above, the unit also covers **Federalist 10** and **Brutus 1** (Topic 1.3), **ratification and the Federalist/Anti-Federalist debate** (1.5), the **principles of the Constitution** — separation of powers, checks and balances (1.6) — and three topics on **federalism** (1.7–1.9): the enumerated, implied and reserved powers, the commerce clause and the necessary-and-proper clause, and the fights over grants-in-aid and mandates.

Those are not written up here yet. When they are, they go in this folder alongside the rest.

## Required documents this unit leans on

Four of the [nine required founding documents](../FOUNDING_DOCUMENTS.md) belong to Unit 1: the **Declaration of Independence**, the **Articles of Confederation**, **Federalist 10**, and **Brutus 1** — plus the **Constitution** itself, which every unit uses.
