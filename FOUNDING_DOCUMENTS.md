# The nine required founding documents

The College Board requires **nine** documents for AP U.S. Government and Politics. They are not background reading — the exam quotes them, asks you to identify the argument, and asks you to connect them to course concepts. One free-response question type is built entirely around them.

Four belong to [Unit 1](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/README.md); the Constitution is used by every unit.

| # | Document | Author | Year | Unit | The argument in one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **[Declaration of Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/declaration_of_independence/README.md)** | Thomas Jefferson (Committee of Five) | 1776 | 1 | Rights come before government; government exists by consent to secure them; one that destroys them may be replaced. |
| 2 | **[Articles of Confederation](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/articles_of_confederation/README.md)** | Continental Congress (drafting led by John Dickinson) | 1777 / in force 1781 | 1 | The states keep their sovereignty; the national government gets only what they lend it. |
| 3 | **Federalist No. 10** | James Madison | 1787 | 1 | Faction is inevitable; a **large** republic controls it better than a small one, because no single faction can command a majority. |
| 4 | **Brutus No. 1** | "Brutus" (probably Robert Yates) | 1787 | 1 | A republic that large cannot represent anyone; the necessary-and-proper and supremacy clauses will swallow the states. |
| 5 | **U.S. Constitution** | Constitutional Convention | 1787 / ratified 1788 | all | The working machinery: enumerated powers, separation of powers, checks and balances, federalism, amendment. |
| 6 | **Federalist No. 51** | James Madison | 1788 | 1–2 | "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition." Separation of powers plus federalism is a **double security** — because men are not angels. |
| 7 | **Federalist No. 70** | Alexander Hamilton | 1788 | 2 | Energy in the executive requires a **single** president, not a committee: for decision, activity, secrecy, dispatch — and so that blame has somewhere to land. |
| 8 | **Federalist No. 78** | Alexander Hamilton | 1788 | 2 | The judiciary is the "least dangerous" branch — no purse, no sword, only judgment — which is why life tenure and judicial review are safe. |
| 9 | **Letter from a Birmingham Jail** | Martin Luther King Jr. | 1963 | 3 | There are just and unjust laws; civil disobedience against an unjust law, openly and accepting the penalty, expresses the highest respect for law. |

## How they pair up

The exam likes documents that argue with each other. Learn them in pairs:

- **Federalist 10 vs Brutus 1** — the ratification debate in miniature. Is a large republic a safeguard against faction, or the end of real representation? Every question about the Federalist / Anti-Federalist split can be answered out of this pair.
- **Federalist 51 vs Brutus 1** — will internal checks actually restrain the national government, or does it need external limits the Constitution does not provide?
- **Declaration vs Letter from a Birmingham Jail** — 187 years apart, the same move. Both appeal to a higher law above the positive law of the moment, and both argue that a government failing that standard may be resisted. King is not attacking the founding ideals; he is holding America to them.
- **Articles vs Constitution** — every clause of the second is an answer to a failure of the first.

## Where to read them

All nine are short, free, and in the public domain.

- **[Declaration of Independence](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript)** · **[Constitution](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript)** · **[Articles of Confederation](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/articles-of-confederation)** — National Archives.
- **[The Federalist Papers, full text](https://guides.loc.gov/federalist-papers/full-text)** — Library of Congress. Numbers 10, 51, 70, 78.
- **[Brutus I](https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/brutus-i/)** — Teaching American History.
- **[Letter from a Birmingham Jail](https://www.africa.upenn.edu/Articles_Gen/Letter_Birmingham.html)** — University of Pennsylvania African Studies Center.
