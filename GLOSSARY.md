# Glossary

Every term the notes and the [Unit 1 pages](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/README.md) use, A–Z, with the page that explains it in full.

Terms carrying a **⚠** are ones the class notes got wrong or loose — see [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md).

| Term | In one line | Full page |
|---|---|---|
| **Annapolis Convention** (1786) | A meeting on interstate trade that only five states attended — its failure produced the call for Philadelphia. | [Articles of Confederation](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/articles_of_confederation/README.md) |
| **Articles of Confederation** | The first U.S. constitution: confederal, one vote per state, no executive, no tax power. Adopted 1777, in force 1781. | [Articles of Confederation](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/articles_of_confederation/README.md) |
| **Ballot initiative** ⚠ | Citizens put a proposed law on the ballot by petition. A **direct**-democracy device, state level only. | [Types of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/types_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Boston Massacre** (1770) | Five colonists killed, six wounded by British soldiers; John Adams then defended the soldiers in court and won. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Boston Tea Party** (1773) ⚠ | Sons of Liberty destroyed 342 chests of tea — civil disobedience **in defence of** "no taxation without representation". | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Brutus 1** | Anti-Federalist essay arguing a republic this large cannot truly represent anyone. A required document. | [Founding documents](FOUNDING_DOCUMENTS.md) |
| **Coercive / Intolerable Acts** (1774) | Britain's punishment of Massachusetts for the Tea Party — which united the other colonies instead of isolating it. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Common Sense** (1776) | Thomas **Paine**'s pamphlet attacking monarchy itself, not just the King's policies. | [Common Sense](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/common_sense/README.md) |
| **Confederal system** | Sovereignty stays with the member states; the central body has only what they grant it. | [Classifications of Government](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/classifications_of_government/README.md) |
| **Consent of the governed** | Government's authority is legitimate only because the people granted it. | [Ideals of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/ideals_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Continental Association** (1774) | The First Continental Congress's boycott of British goods, enforced by local committees. | [First Continental Congress](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/first_continental_congress/README.md) |
| **Declaration and Resolves** (1774) | The First Continental Congress's statement of colonial rights and grievances. | [First Continental Congress](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/first_continental_congress/README.md) |
| **Declaration of Independence** (1776) ⚠ | An announcement to the world, not a petition. Adopted 4 July; signed 2 August. A required document. | [Declaration of Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/declaration_of_independence/README.md) |
| **Dillon's Rule** | Local governments have only the powers their state grants them — which makes every U.S. state internally **unitary**. | [Classifications of Government](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/classifications_of_government/README.md) |
| **Direct (pure) democracy** | The people are the legislature; citizens vote on measures themselves. | [Types of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/types_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Elite democracy** | Model in which a small, wealthy, educated minority holds disproportionate influence. | [Models of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/models_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Faction** | Madison's word in Federalist 10 for a group united by an interest adverse to the rights of others or the common good. | [Types of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/types_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Federal system** | Sovereignty **divided** between national and state levels by a written constitution; both act directly on citizens. | [Classifications of Government](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/classifications_of_government/README.md) |
| **Federalist 10** | Madison: faction is inevitable, and a large republic controls it better than a small one. Required document. | [Founding documents](FOUNDING_DOCUMENTS.md) |
| **First Continental Congress** (1774) | 56 delegates, 12 of 13 colonies, Georgia absent. Did **not** declare independence. | [First Continental Congress](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/first_continental_congress/README.md) |
| **French and Indian War** (1754–63) | Britain won, doubled its debt, and decided the colonies should help pay. The cause of everything after. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Lee Resolution** (2 July 1776) | The motion Congress actually adopted to declare independence — two days before the Declaration's text. | [Declaration of Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/declaration_of_independence/README.md) |
| **Limited government** | Government may do only what it is authorised to do; its power is bounded by law and structure. | [Ideals of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/ideals_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Locke, John** | *Second Treatise* (1689): natural rights to life, liberty and property; consent; the right of revolution. Jefferson's source. | [Ideals of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/ideals_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Natural rights** ⚠ | Rights every **human being** holds by virtue of being human, prior to and independent of government. | [Ideals of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/ideals_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Northwest Ordinance** (1787) | The Confederation's best law: banned slavery in the territory and admitted new states as **equals**. | [Articles of Confederation](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/articles_of_confederation/README.md) |
| **Olive Branch Petition** (1775) ⚠ | The petition to George III that the Declaration is often confused with. He refused to receive it. | [Declaration of Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/declaration_of_independence/README.md) |
| **Paine, Thomas** ⚠ | Author of *Common Sense* (1776). **Paine**, not Payne. | [Common Sense](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/common_sense/README.md) |
| **Participatory democracy** | Model emphasising broad, direct citizen involvement in politics and civil society. | [Models of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/models_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Pluralist democracy** | Model in which policy emerges from bargaining among many competing organised interest groups. | [Models of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/models_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Popular sovereignty** | Ultimate political authority rests with the people. "We the People." | [Ideals of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/ideals_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Proclamation of 1763** | Barred colonial settlement west of the Appalachians — on land the colonists had just helped win. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Recall** | Citizens vote to remove an official before their term ends. State level only. | [Types of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/types_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Referendum** ⚠ | A measure sent to the voters — by the legislature, or by citizen petition to repeal a law. **Direct** democracy. | [Types of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/types_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Representative democracy (republic)** | Citizens elect officials who deliberate and decide for them. The United States, at every level. | [Types of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/types_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Republicanism** | The ideal that the people rule through elected representatives. | [Ideals of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/ideals_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Requisition** | Congress *asking* states for money under the Articles, with no power to compel. It collected about a fifth. | [Articles of Confederation](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/articles_of_confederation/README.md) |
| **Salutary neglect** | Britain's long habit of leaving colonial assemblies alone — which made the post-1763 taxes feel like a new claim. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Second Continental Congress** (1775–81) | Met after Lexington and Concord; ran the war, adopted the Declaration, wrote the Articles. | [Declaration of Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/declaration_of_independence/README.md) |
| **Separation of powers** | Dividing government **by function** — legislative, executive, judicial. Not the same as federalism. | [Classifications of Government](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/classifications_of_government/README.md) |
| **Shays' Rebellion** (1786–87) ⚠ | Indebted farmers shut courts and marched on an arsenal; the Confederation could not respond. Note the apostrophe. | [Shays' Rebellion](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/shays_rebellion/README.md) |
| **Social contract** | People trade some freedom for protection of their rights; a government that breaks the bargain may be replaced. | [Ideals of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/ideals_of_democracy/README.md) |
| **Sons of Liberty** | The organised resistance network formed in 1765 — and the group that staged the Tea Party. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Stamp Act** (1765) | The first direct internal tax on the colonies, hitting lawyers, printers and merchants hardest. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **Unitary system** ⚠ | The central government holds sovereignty; local units exercise **delegated** power it can take back. Not a synonym for tyranny. | [Classifications of Government](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/classifications_of_government/README.md) |
| **Virtual representation** | Parliament's claim that it represented all British subjects everywhere, whether or not they voted. The colonists demanded **actual** representation instead. | [Road to Independence](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/road_to_independence/README.md) |
| **17th Amendment** (1913) | Made senators directly elected. Before it, state legislatures chose them — the best evidence for the elite model. | [Models of Democracy](01_Foundations_of_American_Democracy/models_of_democracy/README.md) |
